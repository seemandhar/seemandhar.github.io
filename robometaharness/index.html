Abstract

Frozen robot policies can fail because of how observations are presented, how action proposals are selected, or how execution is coordinated. Improving these interfaces offers a route to better behavior without updating the policy’s parameters. Discovering a useful intervention, however, requires more than generating plausible wrapper code: it requires compatible interfaces, reproducible comparisons, and evidence that survives independent evaluation.

RoboMetaHarness is an automated research pipeline for discovering training-free harnesses around a user-selected robot policy. It retrieves mechanisms from a research memory, diagnoses recorded failures, proposes falsifiable hypotheses, implements and freezes candidates, and evaluates them against both a baseline and an overhead-matched null control. A staged protocol separates development from fresh validation and full evaluation; negative findings and infrastructure failures return to memory with their costs and limitations.

The project currently contains development screens and reproducibility audits for robot-policy harnesses. These records illustrate why proposal selection, inference precision, and simulator replay must be tested together. The evidence shown here does not establish a validated full-benchmark harness improvement.

How it works

From a robot failure to a testable intervention

A research loop grounded in the policy interface, measured trajectories, and a fixed evaluation contract.

01 / GROUND

Inspect before proposing

Identify the frozen checkpoint, permitted observations, action interface, benchmark, and compute budget. Retrieve relevant mechanisms together with their assumptions and prior failures.

02 / HYPOTHESIZE

Make the mechanism falsifiable

A proposal specifies a failure, an intervention, a predicted effect, a null control, and a cost. Independent review checks whether the mechanism fits the original policy.

03 / IMPLEMENT

Freeze the experiment

Implement a wrapper and pin its source, configuration, cases, and checkpoints before evaluation. Keep policy and auxiliary weights frozen, with official horizons and control timing.

04 / AUDIT

Test the comparison itself

Check initial observations and replay traces before interpreting outcomes. An overhead null performs extra work while preserving the baseline action rule.

05 / EVALUATE

Earn each larger experiment

Start with a small, predeclared development subset. Only complete, valid improvements against both controls can advance to fresh validation and then full evaluation.

06 / LEARN

Retain what failed, and why

Analyze gains, regressions, intervention activations, and inference cost. Preserve negative results and distinguish robot-policy failures from invalid experiments.

Training-free still has a cost. A harness may use additional frozen-model inference. The protocol measures that cost separately from physical interaction, while keeping privileged simulator state and success labels out of the runtime policy.

Why the null control matters

The medoid harness samples three native action chunks and executes the one closest to the others. A sampling null also generates extra proposals but returns the original first proposal and restores RNG state. This separates the action-selection mechanism from the effects of additional inference. Three consistently wrong proposals can still produce the wrong action.
